SECTION 01
Team Strength vs. the Draft Grade
Two different questions, two different answers - and how the Draft Recap grade is actually built.
FF2018
2026 DRAFT RECAP
One more reminder, then we move on

Team Strength (page 2) = best roster, full stop. Draft Recap grade below = best process, given what was on the board. See page 3 for why several teams split the two. Everything from here on is the grade, scored on five weighted factors — when two disagree about a team, this report says which one wins the argument, and why.

How the five draft-grade factors are weighted
Opportunity cost
research + team need
30%
Given real 2026 news and what a team already had at every position, was each notable pick genuinely smart, or just lucky? The factor that separates good process from a good outcome.
Roster construction
balance & risk
25%
Positional waste (hoarding a shallow-need spot while starving a scarce one) and bye-week stacking. Deterministic damage - no amount of luck reverses a four-quarterback roster.
Team strength
starters-weighted value
25%
Value over replacement for the best legal starting nine each team can field - not the whole 16-man roster, which would just reward owning studs who never leave the bench.
Market value
price vs. expectation
15%
Price paid vs. expectation, skill positions only. Real signal, but it measures the price, not the return, so it counts least of the five.
Team identity
strategic coherence
5%
Does the draft read as one coherent plan? Mostly a tiebreaker - it tends to track the other four rather than add new information on its own.
Team need counts as much as name value
  • A pick doesn't lose points just because a "better" player at another position was still on the board - not if that team was already set there.
  • A couple of rosters get real credit for stacking receiver depth in rounds where a tight end was technically available: the question that matters is whether a roster can start nine good players, not whether every pick was the single highest-ranked name on the board.
  • The reverse counts too - a pick loses more when it deepens a position a team already has plenty of while a real hole (usually running back) goes unaddressed.
What "VOR" actually means

VOR (Value Over Replacement) = a player's projected 2026 value minus what a freely-available, replacement-level starter at his position would score instead — how much better is this guy than a waiver-wire fill-in at his spot.

How it's built, step by step
1

Real 2025 points (this league's exact scoring) ÷ games played = a real points-per-game rate.

2

That rate × 17 games = a full-season projection, assuming 2026 health.

3

An injury-risk discount by 2025 games missed: 0-1 none · 2-4 −5% · 5-8 −12% · 9+ −20%. A judgment call, not derived from injury data — stops an 8-game hot streak from grading as a top-3 player with zero risk.

4

Matched to whoever held that player's real 2026 rank (ESPN's Mike Clay, Sept 2026) in the real 2025 table — prices in a role change instead of just last year's box score.

Why rank-matching matters — one real example
Lamar Jackson QB, pick 33
+45.6
Patrick Mahomes QB, pick 108
-32.9
ESPN's Sept 2026 rank has Lamar as the better real 2026 QB, so he comps to a stronger real-2025 producer; Mahomes' lower rank comps to a weaker one — the opposite of a pure "last year's healthy rate" read. This is what "the model rates him ahead/behind" means throughout this report.
Where the numbers come from
  • Market Value and Team Strength (Starters VOR) both come from this league's own points-projection model, described above.
  • Defense and kicker picks are excluded from Market Value - the model was never built to project them like a receiver or running back, so an early or late DST/K pick isn't judged against a number that was never comparable.
  • The rate-plus-injury-discount approach (and its 0/5/12/20% tiers) is a deliberate design choice, not derived purely from data - it separates "bad in 2025" from "hurt but good when playing," while still pricing in real durability risk.
  • Everything else - Research reads, roster-need calls, season outlook - draws on real, current reporting: depth charts, camp notes, injury and role news gathered before this draft.
  • Where the model and the news disagree, the writeup says so rather than picking one silently.

How to read every column
Letter · rank · number

Every column: a grade, that team's rank of 10, then the real underlying number - nothing hidden behind the letter.

Not an arithmetic sum

Final Grade weighs all five per the p2 weights - it isn't a literal average of the five letters shown.

Measured vs. editorial columns
  • Construction, Team Strength, Market Value (65% combined) - fully measured: each team's real number ranked 1st-10th against the other nine, converted to a letter on a standard curve (1st=A down to 10th=D).
  • Opportunity Cost (30%), Identity (5%) - editorial: a football judgment call on whether each notable pick was smart given real 2026 news and team need, and whether the draft reads as one plan. Shown in the same grade-then-rank format for readability, not because it's computed the same way.

Market value vs. roster construction, all ten teams
Market value is the smallest-weighted factor (15%) because it measures price paid, not team quality.
  • Name TBD (Team Marino) ranks 1st overall despite a modest market value (+8, 5th of ten) and a real starting-lineup VOR that's only 6th of ten (+326.9) - they paid close to full price for two lead running backs rather than finding a discount, and several other rosters actually outscored theirs in real 2025 production. They rank 1st because roster construction - the league's best Construction score (1st of 10, zero wasted picks) and best Identity score (1st of 10, one clear disciplined build), on top of a strong Opportunity Cost grade (2nd of 10) - carries the majority of the grade's weight, and none of it depends on which starting lineup scored the most points.
  • Name TBD (Team Melnyczuk) shows a similar pattern: a real Starters VOR that's 4th of ten (+349.5) and fair, unremarkable market value (+1, 8th of ten) still finish only 5th, because three rostered running backs behind Derrick Henry, Cam Skattebo, and David Montgomery - with no real depth if any of them misses time - is a construction risk the whole-roster numbers don't fully price in.
